evcc connects your EV charger, solar inverter, home battery, and energy meters to intelligently charge your vehicle using surplus solar power. It runs entirely on your local network — no cloud account required, no subscription fees for core features.

## What evcc does

evcc sits between your energy devices and makes charging decisions based on real-time data:

- **Solar surplus charging** — charge your EV only when your solar system produces more energy than your home consumes
- **Mixed-mode charging** — combine solar surplus with grid power to guarantee a minimum charge rate
- **Cost-optimized charging** — schedule charging based on dynamic electricity tariffs (Tibber, Octopus, aWATTar, and more)
- **CO₂-optimized charging** — time charging to minimize carbon intensity using grid forecast data
- **Target charging** — set a departure time and desired state of charge; evcc plans the cheapest or greenest charging window automatically

## Architecture

evcc models your home energy setup as a set of connected components:

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Site** | The top-level container. Holds all meters and coordinates loadpoints. |
| **Loadpoint** | A single charging point. Connects one charger (and optionally a meter) to a vehicle. You can have multiple loadpoints. |
| **Meter** | An energy measurement device. Roles include `grid` (import/export), `pv` (solar generation), `battery` (home battery), and `charge` (charge meter at the loadpoint). |
| **Charger** | The physical EV charging device (wallbox). evcc controls its on/off state and current. |
| **Vehicle** | An EV integration that provides state of charge data and supports remote charging control. |
| **Tariff** | A grid pricing or CO₂ forecast integration used for smart scheduling. |

A minimal setup requires at least a grid meter and one loadpoint with a charger. Solar inverter and vehicle integrations are optional but unlock the most value.

## Supported devices

evcc supports a large and growing device ecosystem:

- **200+ EV chargers** — including OCPP and EEBus chargers, as well as smart switches for dumb chargers
- **100+ solar inverters and battery systems** — from Fronius, SMA, Huawei, Growatt, Victron, and many others
- **30+ vehicle brands** — Volkswagen, Tesla, BMW, Renault, Hyundai, Kia, Mercedes-Benz, and more
- **Modbus, HTTP, MQTT, and JavaScript plugins** — integrate any device not covered by built-in templates

See the [evcc device documentation](https://docs.evcc.io/en/docs/devices/chargers) for the full list.

## Charging modes

Each loadpoint operates in one of four charging modes, switchable at any time from the UI or API:

| Mode | Behavior |
|------|----------|
| **Off** | Charging is disabled. |
| **Now** | Charge at maximum available power regardless of solar production. |
| **Min+PV** | Charge at a configurable minimum rate; use additional solar surplus on top. |
| **PV** | Charge only when solar surplus exceeds the minimum charge current. Pauses automatically when surplus drops. |

## Integrations

evcc publishes state to a [REST API](/integrations/rest-api) and [MQTT](/integrations/mqtt), making it easy to connect to home automation platforms:

- **Home Assistant** — official add-on available
- **InfluxDB + Grafana** — built-in data export for energy dashboards
- **Push notifications** — Telegram, Pushover, ntfy, email, and others via a configurable messaging system

## Sponsorship

evcc is open source under the MIT License. Some features — primarily certain vehicle manufacturer APIs that require ongoing reverse-engineering and maintenance effort — require a **sponsor token**. Tokens are available at [sponsor.evcc.io](https://sponsor.evcc.io).

<Note>
  The sponsor token requirement applies per evcc instance. The rest of evcc, including all charger integrations, solar inverter support, and core energy management, is free to use.
</Note>
